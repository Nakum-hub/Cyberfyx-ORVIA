# ORVIA V1 baseline addendum, revision 1.6: DPDPA audit mandate and auditor-driven evidence

**Status:** owner decision recorded 2026-09-29. Additive to the approved master (Rev 1.4) and to the Rev 1.5 addendum (`V1_BASELINE_REV_1_5_AUDIT_EXCHANGE.md`). Where this addendum and Rev 1.5 disagree, this addendum wins **only** on the points under "What changes from 1.5". Everything else in 1.5 and in the master still applies.

**Pending outside engineering:** a lawyer reviews the engagement letter and the data processing agreement against this model before the first real engagement uses it. Until that is recorded, the channel exists in the product but a real client engagement must not be run on it.

## Why

Evidence the client picks and hands over is the weakest kind an auditor can rely on (ISA 500: evidence the auditor obtains directly, or that the system generates under integrity controls, is more reliable than evidence the entity selects). Under Rev 1.5 every piece of evidence was chosen, assembled and released by the client for each package. That made the audit slow and dependent on the client's goodwill, and left the opinion resting on curated material.

Revision 1.6 changes who controls the evidence. The client authorises **once**. From then on, evidence is **generated by ORVIA from its own records**, **signed by the client installation** and **delivered on a schedule and on the auditor's signed request**. The client cannot edit it, leave items out or backdate it.

## Decisions (owner, 2026-09-29)

1. **Audit mandate.** At the start of an engagement the client signs one mandate in ORVIA. It records:
   - the requirements in scope (a subset of the engagement scope);
   - the evidence categories;
   - the collection schedule;
   - the start and end dates;
   - the vendor's audit service address from the installation's trust file.

   A preparer drafts it and an owner or administrator who is a different person approves it. The client can suspend or revoke it at any time; it ends by itself on its end date.
2. **Automatic fulfilment only for personal-data-free evidence.** Inside an active mandate, ORVIA answers scheduled collections and auditor requests by itself, but only in the personal-data-free categories below. Anything else goes to the client approver's queue with a visible due date, including:
   - any evidence file;
   - any sample that could carry personal data;
   - any request outside the mandate.

   Personal data still needs the Rev 1.5 per-item exception and a processing agreement recorded on the vendor side.
3. **Continuous assurance.** After an engagement, the same channel may carry on under a **separate** mandate of kind `CONTINUOUS_ASSURANCE`. It is off by default, needs its own dual approval, lasts at most 400 days and ends automatically.
4. **Portal is the staff login** (Rev 1.5 decision 2, restated): the vendor portal holds staff logins with mandatory MFA. The website's "Vendor / Auditor login" is `https://<vendor-domain>/vendor/sign-in`: one login, one session.
5. **The file fallback stays** for clients whose network does not allow outbound HTTPS to the vendor. It uses the Rev 1.5 sealed package, exported and uploaded by a client user.

## What changes from 1.5

| Rev 1.5 | Rev 1.6 |
|---|---|
| "File export and manual upload only. There is no network transport … and the customer installation never calls the vendor." | The customer installation may make **outbound HTTPS calls to exactly one address**: the vendor audit service named in its trust file. It makes them only while a mandate is active. Nothing is ever inbound. |
| "The vendor never pulls data." | The vendor still never connects to, signs in to or reads a client installation. The auditor **may request** evidence by a signed request that the client's ORVIA collects on its own check-in. ORVIA answers it automatically only inside the mandate and only in personal-data-free categories. |
| Every package released by a person. | A person releases the **mandate**. Deliveries under it are automatic and each is recorded. The per-package release remains for evidence files and exceptions. |

Nothing else changes. In particular: DPDPA only; no certification wording; opinion as of a date for a stated scope; dual control; the vendor-visibility screen lists everything that has left; purge after the engagement closes.

## Evidence categories allowed for automatic fulfilment

Every category is an aggregate or a state read from ORVIA's own records under row-level security. None carries identifiers, contact details or free text written by a data principal.

| Category | What is produced |
|---|---|
| `INDICATORS` | The aggregate indicators per requirement (`backend/domain/src/dpdpa-audit/indicators.ts`): counts and dates, each with its basis. |
| `CONTROL_STANDING` | Each control mapped to the DPDP framework: its standing, evidence date, review decision and validity. It gives no evidence content. |
| `CONTROL_TESTS` | Results of scheduled control tests over the period (pass/fail/drift counts per test) and the latest result. |
| `NOTICE_VERSIONS` | Published notice versions: identifier, version, language, publication date and content digest. |
| `POLICY_VERSIONS` | Policy documents: identifier, version, state and approval date. |
| `ACTIVITY_LOG_DIGEST` | For the period, the count of audit events per operation family and a SHA-256 over the ordered event identifiers. It carries no actors and no resource identifiers. |
| `SAMPLE_COUNTS` | Answers to auditor sampling requests over the allowlisted populations, as counts over the auditor's seeded selection (see below). No record content is sent. |

Record-level samples (for example, a redacted consent record) are **not** automatic. They go to the client approver as a Rev 1.5 package item.

## Tamper evidence

- Each delivery (a *snapshot* or a *response*) is canonical JSON. It is signed with the **client installation's evidence key** (Ed25519), generated in the installation when the first mandate is approved. The private key is never shown and never leaves the installation.
- Each delivery names the digest of the previous delivery on the same channel. A missing, repeated or altered delivery breaks the chain, and the vendor reports the break.
- The vendor pins the installation's public key the first time a check-in authenticated with the engagement secret presents it. A later change of key is refused and shown to the auditor.
- **Limit, stated plainly:** the client's own audit event table is not hash-chained. `ACTIVITY_LOG_DIGEST` proves what the log contained when each snapshot was taken, and the chain between snapshots proves nothing was withdrawn afterwards. It does not prove the log was never altered before the first snapshot.

## Channel protocol

- **Engagement secret.** The one-time engagement code the vendor issues (Rev 1.5) derives a channel key: `K = HMAC-SHA256(key = normalised code, "orvia-audit-channel-v1")`.
  - The vendor keeps `K` sealed with its vault key.
  - The client keeps `K` in a table only the background worker can read.
  - Neither side keeps the code.
- **Check-in (client → vendor, outbound).**
  - The worker calls `POST /api/v1/vendor/channel/check-in` on the address in the trust file. It sends the engagement digest, a timestamp and `HMAC(K, timestamp + "." + sha256(body))`.
  - The body carries the installation's evidence public key, the signed mandate and acknowledgements of requests already answered.
  - The vendor answers with the pending auditor requests, signed with the vendor **audit** key. The client verifies that signature with the audit public key in its trust file, so an answer from anyone else is ignored.
- **Delivery (client → vendor, outbound).** `POST /api/v1/vendor/channel/deliveries`, authenticated the same way. The body is the delivery signed by the installation evidence key. The vendor returns a **receipt** signed with the audit key, naming the delivery digest and the outcome.
  - A timeout is recorded as UNKNOWN and retried.
  - The vendor answers a repeated delivery with the original receipt, so a retry never duplicates anything.
- **Auditor requests** are signed by the vendor audit key. The kinds are:
  - `COLLECT_NOW`: an extra snapshot of named categories;
  - `SAMPLE_COUNT`: a population key from the allowlist, a size and the auditor's seed;
  - `EVIDENCE_FILE`: always sent to the client approver.

  The client refuses a request it cannot verify, one for another engagement, one outside the mandate or one past its due date. It records the refusal and reports it at the next check-in.
- **Sampling.** The auditor chooses the seed. The client's ORVIA selects deterministically: members of the population are ordered by `HMAC-SHA256(seed, record id)` and the first *n* are taken, so the client cannot cherry-pick. Only counts and pass/fail over the selection are returned automatically.
- **Only while active.**
  - The worker makes no call when no mandate is active.
  - The vendor refuses every channel call for an engagement that is closed, or whose mandate has ended, been suspended or been revoked (the mandate state is reported at check-in and signed by the client).
  - A continuous-assurance mandate keeps a channel open only for its own dates.

## Visibility

- **Client:** the vendor-visibility screen lists every check-in, every delivery and every refused request, with digests and times. The DPDPA audit screen shows the mandate, the channel health, the auditor's requests and whether each was answered automatically or is waiting for approval.
- **Vendor:** the engagement screen shows:
  - the mandate exactly as the client signed it;
  - channel health (last check-in, chain intact or broken, key pinned);
  - the evidence timeline;
  - requests and their answers;
  - client response times.

  Unanswered requests past their due date go into the report as scope limitations.
- **Vendor leadership overview (super administrator and administrator):** counts only, from real records:
  - clients and licence status;
  - licences expiring;
  - engagements by stage;
  - channel health;
  - requests overdue with clients;
  - findings by severity and overdue;
  - reports signed;
  - support queue.

  It gives no access to evidence content; that stays with the engagement team.

## Security principles (unchanged, restated)

- Server-side authorisation, least privilege, row-level security, and audit of every action on both installations.
- Durable PostgreSQL state for every check-in, delivery, attempt and receipt. No in-memory queue. An acknowledgement is not verification: only a receipt that verifies against the audit key moves a delivery to RECEIVED.
- The client's outbound address comes only from the trust file the installer placed. HTTPS is required, except to loopback in development. Redirects are not followed and responses are size-limited.

## Out of scope for this revision

- Payment activation, and pricing of continuous assurance.
- The public website build: only its link to `/vendor/sign-in` is in scope.
- SSO/SCIM.
- A hash-chained client audit log (a possible later change, listed as a limit above).
